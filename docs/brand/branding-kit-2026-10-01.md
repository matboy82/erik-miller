# Branding Kit: Miller Remodeling LLC — 2026-10-01

> **Status: Approved — Matt, 2026-10-01; Erik's direction pick remains a delivery dependency.** This kit exists for one reason: the site must look like *Erik's company*, not like every AI-generated contractor template. Three creative directions are specified below with full tokens; **Erik picks one**, it locks like the stack ADR, and Codex builds against it.

## 1. Brand truth (who Erik is, in one paragraph)

Erik Miller has spent 25 years remodeling Treasure Valley homes — kitchens, baths, additions, whole-home work — and systematized the parts most contractors wing: an in-house designer, floorplans and 3D walkthroughs before a single wall opens, selections handled, timelines kept. His reviews repeat the same themes: the work transformed the space, he communicated, he finished on time and on budget — including a church that hired him three times. He is done giving design away for free and done with 33 small jobs a year; he wants eight $100k, three-month projects with clients who buy the design process up front.

**The brand idea:** *the contractor who designs it before he builds it.* Trust + craft + process, in that order.

## 2. Positioning & tagline options

**Positioning statement:** For Treasure Valley homeowners planning a major kitchen, bath, or whole-home remodel, Miller Remodeling is the design-build remodeler that sells you the design first — floorplans, selections, and 3D walkthroughs — so the build runs on time, on budget, and without surprises.

**Tagline candidates** (pick one with the direction):
1. "Designed first. Built right."
2. "See it before we build it."
3. "25 years. One process. Zero surprises."
4. "Kitchens and baths, designed around your life."
5. "The remodel starts on paper — not in your kitchen."

## 3. Ideal customer avatar

**Meet "The Hendersons"** — Karen (52) and Tom (55), Eagle. $780k home bought in 2008, original 1990s kitchen and primary bath. Tom's an engineer, Karen runs the household decisions. Combined HHI ~$220k.

- **What they want:** a kitchen transformation ($90–120k) and eventually the primary bath. They've saved for this for five years; it's the one big project.
- **What they fear:** the contractor who disappears mid-job (their neighbors lived it), dust and chaos for six months, the budget doubling, and having to make 200 tile decisions alone while working full-time.
- **What wins them:** seeing the finished kitchen in 3D *before* demo day. One accountable team. An in-house designer who handles selections. A written process with a timeline. Reviews that say "on time and on budget" — from people like them.
- **Their objection:** "Why am I paying for design?" → The reframe the whole brand carries: *the design fee is what keeps a $100k project from becoming a $140k surprise.* (This is Erik's business model and the site's conversion argument.)
- **Where they look:** Google ("kitchen remodeler Eagle ID"), Google reviews, Facebook community groups, Houzz, referrals from friends at church.

**Secondary avatar:** the ADU/mother-in-law buyer (aging parent moving closer — Star/Middleton, budget-conscious but process-hungry) and the whole-home updater (Boise bench, 1970s home, $150k+).

**What this means for design:** the site should feel calm, established, and expensive-in-the-right-way — never cheap, never flashy, never techy. Karen should feel *relief* within ten seconds: "these people have a process."

## 4. Voice & tone

- **Plain-spoken Idaho direct.** Short sentences. No jargon, no "synergy," no "passion for creating spaces."
- **Confident, not boastful.** Proof over promises: "11 reviews, 5.0 stars" beats "we're the best."
- **Process-forward.** Every claim gets a mechanism: not "quality craftsmanship" but "floorplans and 3D walkthroughs before demo day."
- **Warm but not folksy.** Respect the $100k decision.

**Do:** "You'll approve the design — floorplans, selections, and a 3D walkthrough — before we touch a wall."
**Don't:** "We bring your dream space to life with our passion for excellence."

## 5. Creative directions (Erik picks one)

### Direction A — Heritage Craftsman *(recommended)*

The 25-year story, told with warmth. Established, trustworthy, quietly premium. Feels like the company Karen already trusts.

- **Palette:**
  - `--brand-ink: #1B2420` — deep evergreen-charcoal (headlines, footer)
  - `--brand-paper: #F6F1E7` — warm bone (page background)
  - `--brand-brass: #A8824A` — aged brass (accents, rules, CTAs hover)
  - `--brand-clay: #9C4A2F` — fired clay (primary CTA, sparing)
  - `--brand-sage: #7D8B7A` — muted sage (secondary text, borders)
  - `--brand-stone: #E8E0D0` — cut stone (cards, dividers)
- **Typography:** *Fraunces* (display serif, headlines — warmth + craft) + *Inter* (body/UI). Headlines set tight, sentence case, never all-caps.
- **Logo direction:** wordmark "Miller Remodeling" in a refined serif with a monogram **M built from a miter joint** — two 45° cuts forming the letter. Single color (ink or brass). No house icon, no roofline cliché, no hammer.
- **Texture:** subtle paper grain on backgrounds, hairline brass rules, generous whitespace. Photography does the heavy lifting.
- **UI feel:** calm, editorial. Big before/after imagery, process timeline as a visual spine, reviews pulled close to every CTA.

### Direction B — Modern Design-Build Studio

Architectural confidence. The "sell the design process" story at full volume — for the buyer who wants a studio, not a contractor.

- **Palette:**
  - `--brand-graphite: #23262B` — graphite (headlines, dark sections)
  - `--brand-paper: #FAF8F3` — warm paper (background)
  - `--brand-amber: #C97A1F` — jobsite amber (CTA, accents — restrained, never safety-cone)
  - `--brand-steel: #5B6470` — steel gray (secondary text)
  - `--brand-line: #E4DFD4` — hairline dividers
- **Typography:** *Archivo* (headlines, with Expanded weights for hero) + *Inter* (body). More uppercase kickers, tighter tracking, grid-forward layouts.
- **Logo direction:** geometric wordmark, all-caps "MILLER" with "REMODELING" letterspaced beneath; monogram **M as a floorplan corner** — walls meeting at 90°. Ink or amber on paper.
- **Texture:** blueprint-grid motifs (very subtle), precise hairlines, numbered process steps (01–05) as a design element.
- **UI feel:** structured, precise. The 3D walkthrough and floorplans are hero content; the process page is the second-most important page on the site.

### Direction C — Quiet Luxury

For the buyer whose home is already beautiful and wants it handled accordingly. Minimal, warm, high-end — the most expensive-feeling of the three.

- **Palette:**
  - `--brand-espresso: #1E1B17` — espresso (text, dark sections)
  - `--brand-linen: #F3EEE5` — linen (background)
  - `--brand-bronze: #8C6A45` — bronze (accents, hairlines)
  - `--brand-fog: #A39E93` — warm fog (secondary text)
  - `--brand-cream: #EAE3D5` — cream (cards)
- **Typography:** *Newsreader* (light serif, headlines — editorial, calm) + *Inter* (body). Lots of air; small, letterspaced kickers.
- **Logo direction:** delicate serif wordmark, letterspaced; monogram **M as a single-line drawing** — continuous-line mark. Bronze on linen.
- **Texture:** almost none — whitespace is the luxury. Full-bleed finished photography, minimal chrome.
- **UI feel:** gallery-like. Portfolio is the homepage's center of gravity; copy is short and assured.

## 6. Recommendation

**Direction A — Heritage Craftsman.** It fits the evidence: 25 years, church client three times over, "on time and on budget" reviews — that's a heritage trust story, not a startup-studio story. It differentiates from both the cheap handyman (who can't afford this palette) and the cold luxury builder, and it photographs beautifully against real job-site imagery. It can borrow B's process-forward structure (numbered steps, floorplan/3D as hero content) without wearing B's harder edge.

## 7. Photography & art direction (all directions)

**Rule zero: real jobs only. No stock photography of smiling contractors with crossed arms.** Every image on the site should be traceable to a Miller Remodeling project.

**Development exception approved by Matt, 2026-10-01:** licensed online stock assets and clearly marked Draft writeups may be used in development/test builds while Erik supplies photos. Label them as development placeholders, record sources/licenses and replacement ownership in `PLACEHOLDERS.md`, and make photos/writeups replaceable without changing page layouts. The real-jobs-only rule governs production; stock assets must not be presented as Miller projects or shipped as portfolio proof.

- **The shot list per job:** before (honest, unstaged) → during (craft in progress: tile lines, joinery, designer reviewing plans on site) → after (finished space, natural light) → finished-product video (60–90s slow walkthrough, no music-video editing).
- **People:** Erik and the designer at work — reviewing plans at a kitchen table, walking a client through the 3D model. Candid over posed. Hands and materials close-ups (tile, wood grain, hardware) as texture.
- **The 3D walkthrough** is a brand asset: screen-capture fly-throughs of the model next to the finished room ("this is what we showed them before demo day").
- **Tone:** warm natural light, honest job sites (drop cloths are fine — they're proof of care), no fisheye listing-photo look.

## 8. UI/UX principles (for Codex)

1. **Calm > clever.** No animation for its own sake. Motion only to explain (process timeline, before/after slider).
2. **Proof near action.** Every CTA section carries a review, a stat, or a project — never a naked button.
3. **The process is the product.** The 5-step process (consult → design → selections → build → walkthrough) is a visual spine across the site, not a buried page.
4. **Design-fee framing everywhere it matters:** wizard, consult booking, and process page all carry the "design first" argument in one line.
5. **Mobile-first, thumb-friendly.** Karen browses on her phone at 9pm. Tap targets, readable type, fast loads.
6. **Accessibility is brand:** WCAG 2.1 AA isn't a checkbox — an inaccessible site contradicts "we sweat the details."

## 9. Anti-patterns (never ship)

- Purple/blue gradients, glassmorphism, floating SaaS cards, glow effects.
- Generic Unsplash construction workers, hard hats crossed-arms hero shots.
- "Passion for creating spaces" / "dream home" / "excellence" filler copy.
- Chatbot popup, exit-intent spam, fake urgency ("only 3 slots left!").
- Emoji bullets, stock icons for services (draw simple line icons or use none).
- Lorem ipsum anywhere Erik can see it — placeholder copy must read as *draft real copy*, marked DRAFT.

## 10. Design tokens for Codex

- Tokens live at `apps/web/src/styles/tokens.css` as CSS custom properties, one `:root` block per direction during exploration, **one block after Erik's pick**.
- **Test-URL theme switcher** (PRD WS-0): during exploration all three `:root` blocks ship on the test build, switched via `data-theme` on `<html>` from the design-review toolbar (env-gated, never in production). Content variants (tagline candidates + hero copy) switch alongside the theme so Erik judges words and look together.
- Fonts: Google Fonts (self-host at build; no runtime font CDN dependency). Display font only on headlines — body is always Inter for readability.
- Spacing: 8px base scale. Radius: small (4–6px) — craftsman, not bubbly. Shadows: minimal, warm-tinted.
- The direction pick locks: palette hexes, type pairing, logo concept, and the tagline choice. Record it in the ADR pattern (new ADR or ADR amendment) so it's as durable as the stack decision.
